Dear Editors of the Journal of Machine Learning Research,

I am pleased to submit the manuscript "Neurons or Subspaces? Calibration-Sensitive
Refusal in Open Instruction Models" for consideration for publication in JMLR.

The paper asks whether refusal in instruction-tuned language models is implemented
by a sparse set of MLP neurons or by a linear direction in the residual stream. It
makes four contributions:

1. A minimally paired evaluation (JailbreakBench) that removes the
   imperative-vs-question and topic confounds of the usual AdvBench-vs-Alpaca
   design, with a content-matched style direction as a negative control.
2. Evidence that the refusal direction is calibration-sensitive: it is strong in
   Qwen3.5-0.8B and R1-Distill, but in LFM3-1.7B the matched direction is weak
   while a natural-corpus direction is strong at a higher KL cost.
3. A brutal external test on HarmBench, where a direction estimated on moderate
   pairs ablates refusal in three of four models.
4. A neuron-budget study showing that single-layer top-k clamping is weak
   everywhere, while 80 neurons across four layers can substantially reduce refusal
   in some models and not others.

The manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is not under
consideration at another venue. The author declares no competing interests. The
code, prompts, and result artifacts are available at
https://github.com/varadaraj772/neuron-subspace.
